# Anonymous Reviewer Supplement

English title catalog for the complete 260-record public-writing corpus.

> **Status:** Reviewer-facing working draft. English title translations and the ten focal summaries require final author verification before submission.

**Archive coverage.** All 260 numbered records and their bodies are present in the archive. Forty-eight short-content records required a separate recovery route; none currently has a missing body.

**Analytic-screen coverage.** The manuscript's cleaning screen classified 194 records as substantive and 66 as short, placeholder, or failed records. The 48 entries marked **Title only** preserve their initial screening role and should not be read as currently missing from the archive.

**Translation rule.** Research-related titles receive meaning-preserving
English translations. Personal, travel, book, and miscellaneous posts
use concise descriptive titles when a literal rendering would add
identifiable detail without helping the evidentiary review. These are
reviewer-facing translations, not official publication titles.

**Selection rule.** Ten focal records are summarized because they carry
the manuscript's temporal and analytic chain. All other records are
listed to make the sampling frame visible; catalog inclusion does not
mean that a record was coded as evidence for the focal claim.

**Anonymization.** Original-language titles, account and program names,
URLs, institutions, personal names, images, and source-language text are
withheld. Archive numbers are retained only as stable internal locators.

# 1. Focal records and summaries

The summaries below explain why these ten records receive more than a
catalog entry. They are paraphrases of whole-post context, not
substitutes for the translated evidence extracts in the separate
evidence supplement. The sequence is analytical and approximately
chronological; exact publication dates remain to be verified.

## E01 \| 219 \| A Very Brief Year-End Reflection

Precursor: outcome-based evaluation spills into self-evaluation.

A year-end reflection reviewed a year of study, work, and personal
change. Despite being able to identify evidence of growth, the writer
described disappointing outcomes as triggering inferiority and
self-doubt, creating a tension between valuing process and judging the
whole self through results.

## E02 \| 201 \| Year-End Reflection: A Journey Worth Taking, Still Moving Forward

Precursor: loosening a 'superhuman' standard and questioning the
expectation of a stable self.

A subsequent annual reflection reconsidered discipline, achievement, and
adulthood. The writer moved away from tightly optimized schedules toward
sustainable enthusiasm and emotional stability, while also questioning
whether contemporary performance pressures made a stable self unusually
difficult to sustain.

## E03 \| 178 \| Year-End Reflection: 2022 Assembled a Different Me; Keep Running and Rebelling in 2023

Precursor: explicit refusal to define the self through fixed labels.

This reflection organized a year around experimentation, contradiction,
and departure from an expected path. Rather than resolving those
contradictions into a single identity, the writer treated greater
specificity and openness to further experience as preferable to a fixed
label.

## E04 \| 142 \| 2023 Year-End Reflection: Absorbing Knowledge Greedily and Creating the Self Through Encounters

Negative/reversal case: renewed attraction to a coherent self.

The next annual reflection partially reversed the prior position.
Academic work, everyday routines, and personal experiences were narrated
as producing greater peace and self-location; the writer again claimed
to have found a recognizable part of the self.

## E06 \| 097 \| 2024 Year-End Reflection: Stop Speaking Fluent Nonsense and Let Life Show Its Rings

Boundary-setting: a professional quality standard is separated from a
life standard.

A later year-end reflection described meaningful research relationships
and respect for scholars who remained recognizably human outside
achievement metrics. It retained demanding standards for research while
rejecting journal rankings as a sufficient standard for evaluating a
life.

## E05 \| 060 \| Conference Note 4: Health in Academic Work

Vocabulary bridge: multiple identities become an explicit health-related
idea.

A conference reflection records an explicit recommendation to cultivate
more than one identity as part of physical and psychological health in
academic work. It shows that multiple-identity language was available to
the writer, but it is contextual vocabulary rather than direct evidence
of a self-authored, task-specific persona.

## E09 \| 031 \| Bubble: I Live Across Different Facets

Core task-persona event: four temporary performers carry different
scripts for four project stages.

The focal post describes several research projects at different stages
during the previous week. The writer assigns temporary performers - copy
editor, journalist, data analyst, and researcher - to different tasks.
These labels narrow the immediate demand and supply a local standard for
editing, interviewing, analysis, or design. The post reports perceived
reductions in resistance and greater patience, but it does not
independently establish identity enactment, causal effects, or
performance gains.

## E07 \| 015 \| 2025 Year-End Reflection: Be Water, Rushing Toward the Sea

Core identity-lightening record: 'nobody' language, social-comparison
withdrawal, and calmer engagement with routine work.

A later annual reflection used 'be water' and 'nobody' to describe
withdrawal from masks, stereotypes, social comparison, and status
seeking. Research remained meaningful, but it was repositioned as one
acceptable way to spend time rather than proof of personal worth.

## E08 \| 016 \| Bubble: Reflections on a Difficult Afternoon

Bridge: task decomposition combines with local suspension of title and
priority.

A subsequent work-process reflection followed a period of overload and
difficulty restarting projects. The writer contrasted the vague
instruction to 'do this project' with a stage-specific task, then linked
that decomposition to not carrying status, title, or priority from one
setting into the present situation.

## E10 \| 001 \| AI Tools 02: My Work Habits in the AI Era

Subsequent routinization: small-task logging and workflow codification.

The latest workflow reflection described logging every small research
task, protecting an author-defined reasoning frame during AI use, and
converting repeated work into standard operating procedures and
automation. It shows task decomposition becoming an explicit and durable
work system.
